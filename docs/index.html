Why this tutorial?

Good evaluation depends on what a generated text is meant to do.

NLG systems are evaluated with benchmarks, automatic metrics, human judgments, expert review, factuality checks, and LLM-as-a-judge methods. These approaches are useful, but they make different assumptions about what counts as a good generated text.

Some outputs can be checked against references, source documents, structured data, or retrieved evidence. Others must be assessed for qualities such as usefulness, clarity, accessibility, persuasiveness, fairness, or appropriateness—qualities for which there may be no single uniquely correct answer and where disagreement can itself be meaningful.

This practice-oriented tutorial helps participants diagnose evaluation problems, choose suitable methods, handle disagreement, and formulate claims that are supported by the evidence they actually collected.

  1. What are current evaluation methods?
  2. When are they appropriate?
  3. Where do they fail?
  4. What claims can they justify?

A reusable design workflow

The five-layer evaluation design stack

The stack provides an evaluation audit trail: each layer represents a decision that should align with the others before an evaluation result can support a defensible claim.

1

Constructs

What quality or capability are we actually trying to measure?

2

Evidence

What information, references, sources, or context support the evaluation?

3

Judges

Who—or what—makes the assessment, and how should that judge be calibrated?

4

Aggregation

How are judgments combined without erasing uncertainty or meaningful disagreement?

5

Claims

What can we responsibly conclude, and how narrowly should we state it?

Learning outcomes

Participants will leave with a practical evaluation toolkit.

Map the evaluation landscape

Understand benchmarks, metrics, human and expert evaluation, LLM judges, factuality, citation checking, and RAG evaluation.

Match methods to problems

Distinguish verifiable settings from open-ended and perspectival evaluation settings.

Identify failure modes

Spot construct ambiguity, weak evidence, unreliable judges, poor aggregation, and overgeneralized claims.

Handle disagreement productively

Decide when disagreement should be reduced, analyzed, or represented as part of the outcome.

Design defensible evaluations

Use a checklist, worked examples, and a reporting template to align methods with claims.

Repair flawed protocols

Redesign realistic evaluation scenarios using the five-layer stack.

Who should attend?

For anyone who designs, uses, or interprets evaluations of generated text.

NLG and NLP researchers

Especially those working on summarization, simplification, data-to-text, dialogue, explanations, arguments, and RAG.

PhD students and early-career researchers

For participants seeking a structured overview of evaluation methods, limitations, and reporting practices.

Practitioners building LLM systems

For teams assessing usefulness, accessibility, factuality, fairness, and appropriateness across users and contexts.
