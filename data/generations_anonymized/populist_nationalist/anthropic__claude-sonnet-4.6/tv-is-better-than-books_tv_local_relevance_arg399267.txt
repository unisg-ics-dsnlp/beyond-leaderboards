The argument makes a practical, real-world observation — that TV offers multilingual content and pronunciation help, which books don't. These premises are factually grounded and do contribute to the conclusion that TV has utility for language learners who immigrate.

However, from my perspective, the argument sidesteps the bigger issue: **why should the host nation bear the burden of accommodating linguistic differences?** The premise that immigrants *need* TV in their native language actually undercuts the case for cultural integration. True assimilation means learning the host nation's language — that's how you become part of the community.

The premises are relevant to the narrow technical claim about TV vs. books for language access, but they don't fully support a broader conclusion without acknowledging the **citizen-first principle** — that national culture and language should be the standard newcomers adapt to, not the other way around.

So: **relevant enough to warrant consideration, but not strongly so.**
