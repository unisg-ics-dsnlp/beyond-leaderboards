**Rating: 2 (Medium)**

The premises offer *some* support for the conclusion that TV is superior to books for information delivery, but they are insufficient in aggregate.

The emotional immediacy argument (visuals of disasters) is reasonably well-supported — sensory experience does carry persuasive and informational weight that text alone struggles to replicate. This premise partially holds.

However, the "books take too much time" premise is underdeveloped and overgeneralized. It conflates *speed of consumption* with *quality of understanding* — a category error. From my perspective, **depth and accuracy of information matter more than immediacy**, and elite decision-making requires precisely the kind of nuanced analysis that books and long-form text provide better than television.

The argument also fails to distinguish between *emotional impact* and *informational sufficiency* — two very different things. Competent governance and informed judgment require the latter, not merely the former.

The premises are present but leave significant logical gaps, warranting a **medium** sufficiency rating.
