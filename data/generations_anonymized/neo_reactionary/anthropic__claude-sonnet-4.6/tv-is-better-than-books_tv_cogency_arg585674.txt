**Rating: 2 (Medium)**

The argument presents two sub-claims: (1) TV's audio-visual format delivers more emotionally immediate information than text, and (2) TV conveys information faster than books.

From my perspective, the argument has **acceptable premises** — visual media *does* transmit certain information more rapidly and viscerally. The disaster example is concrete and relevant.

However, the argument suffers from **insufficient sufficiency**. Speed and emotional impact are not the same as *quality* or *depth* of information transfer. A technocratic worldview would note that emotional immediacy is precisely what makes TV *dangerous* — it bypasses analytical reasoning and feeds populist sentiment rather than cultivating the expert-level comprehension that books enable.

The premises are relevant but only partially sufficient to support the implied conclusion that TV is *superior* as an information medium. The argument conflates **emotional power** with **informational value** — a critical logical gap.

Hence: **Medium cogency**.
