    Global Sufficiency: An argumentation should be seen as globally sufficient if it adequately rebuts those counter-arguments to its conclusion that can be anticipated. Notice that it is not generally clear which and how many counter-arguments can be anticipated. There may be cases where it is infeasible to rebut all such objections. **Global Sufficiency: 1 (Low)**

This argument fails global sufficiency. It asserts economic harm (jobs, corporate losses) from a ban but does not anticipate or rebut core counter-arguments: environmental externalities (plastic pollution as potential rights violations via property damage), claims that tap water substitutes freely without net loss, creative destruction/offsetting gains elsewhere in a free market, or that consumer demand would simply shift voluntarily.  

Worse, it is internally inconsistent ("hurt the economy severely" vs. "only hurt big corporations") and relies on a biased corporate source without addressing legitimacy of holdings or consent. From a libertarian view, the decisive objection is that any ban initiates force against self-owners, property, and voluntary exchange—none of which this economic claim even engages, let alone rebuts. A rights-based case under the non-aggression principle would be far stronger; this one is not.
