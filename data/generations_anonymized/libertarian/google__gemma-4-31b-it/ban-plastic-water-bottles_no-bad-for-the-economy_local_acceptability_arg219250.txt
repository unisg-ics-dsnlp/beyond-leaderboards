The second premise—that bottles can be reused—is a factual claim about property and utility, which is highly acceptable. However, the first premise—that bottled water is a "waste"—is a subjective value judgment. From a libertarian perspective, "waste" is determined by the owner's preferences; if a consumer voluntarily exchanges money for bottled water, it is a rational allocation of resources, not a waste. Therefore, the aggregate acceptability is lowered by the implicit assumption of a collective standard of utility.
