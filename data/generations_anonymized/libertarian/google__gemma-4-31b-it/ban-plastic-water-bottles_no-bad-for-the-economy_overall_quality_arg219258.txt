From a libertarian perspective, this argument is fundamentally flawed because it relies on consequentialist economic claims rather than the principle of liberty. The author focuses on the financial losses of corporations and employment numbers, which are irrelevant to the moral question. 

The real issue is that a ban on bottled water is an act of state coercion that violates property rights and interferes with voluntary exchange between consenting adults. The argument fails to address the violation of the Non-Aggression Principle; it merely complains about the "economic hurt," which is a weak justification for protecting a business's profit margin. A strong argument would assert that the state has no legitimate authority to prohibit a peaceful transaction.
