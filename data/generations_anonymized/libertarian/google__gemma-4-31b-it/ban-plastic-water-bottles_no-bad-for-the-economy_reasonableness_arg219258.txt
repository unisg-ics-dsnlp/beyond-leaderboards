From a Libertarian perspective, this argument is fundamentally flawed because it relies on "economic harm" and corporate losses to oppose a ban. The loss of profits for Nestle or the disappearance of specific jobs are irrelevant to the moral question. 

The real issue is the **initiation of force**: a ban on bottled water is an act of state coercion that violates property rights and the principle of voluntary exchange. The author fails to argue that the ban is an illegitimate interference with self-ownership and contractual freedom, focusing instead on utilitarian outcomes. An argument against coercion should be based on rights, not on whether a corporation loses money.
