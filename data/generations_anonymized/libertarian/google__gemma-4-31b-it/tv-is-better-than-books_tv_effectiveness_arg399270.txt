The author relies on fallacies and a misunderstanding of market dynamics. First, the claim that actors wouldn't have jobs without TV ignores the reality of voluntary exchange; actors would simply find other venues (theater, film, digital media) to offer their labor. Second, the claim regarding information flow is a "false necessity" argument. The market provides numerous competing ways to disseminate news; the absence of one medium does not preclude the existence of others. Finally, the argument appeals to a perceived "public good" to justify the existence of a medium, but fails to acknowledge that these services are most efficient when provided voluntarily, not as an essential utility. It is an emotional plea, not a logical defense of necessity.
