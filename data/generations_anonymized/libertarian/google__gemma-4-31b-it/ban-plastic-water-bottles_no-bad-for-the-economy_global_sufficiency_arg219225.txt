Rating: 2 (Medium)

From a libertarian perspective, the conclusion—that banning bottled water is a mistake—is correct because a ban is an act of state coercion that violates property rights and the principle of voluntary exchange.

However, the author's argumentation is globally insufficient because it relies on "helping the economy" (a collectivist utility argument) rather than the fundamental right of individuals to trade goods without interference. While the author addresses the environmental counter-argument via recycling data, they fail to address the core moral objection: that the state has no legitimate authority to prohibit a consensual transaction between competent adults. The conclusion is right, but the reasoning is based on convenience and economics rather than the Non-Aggression Principle.
