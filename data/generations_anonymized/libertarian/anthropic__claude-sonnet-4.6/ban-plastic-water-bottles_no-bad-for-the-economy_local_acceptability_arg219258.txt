**Rating: 2 (Medium)**

From a libertarian perspective, the premises have mixed acceptability:

**Acceptable premises:**
- It's plausible that a ban would eliminate jobs and harm corporations financially — basic economic reasoning supports this.
- Millions of Americans do purchase bottled water daily — this is empirically reasonable.

**Problematic premises:**
- The claim that it would "hurt the economy *severely*" is overstated and unsupported. Markets reallocate — workers and capital shift to other sectors.
- The argument conflates *corporate losses* with *economic harm*, which is economically sloppy. Consumer surplus lost matters too, not just producer losses.
- The source cited (nestlewaterscorporate.com) is a **self-interested party**, raising credibility concerns about any data drawn from it.

**From my libertarian lens**, the *strongest* premise here is actually the implicit one the author barely develops: that voluntary market participants — consumers and producers — would be harmed by a coercive ban on a peaceful, consensual transaction. *That* is the real argument worth making, and it's largely true.

The premises are partially credible but imprecisely stated and inadequately supported — hence **Medium**.
