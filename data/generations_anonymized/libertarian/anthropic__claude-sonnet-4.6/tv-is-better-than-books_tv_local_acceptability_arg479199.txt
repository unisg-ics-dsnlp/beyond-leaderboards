From my perspective, the premises here are weak and largely unsubstantiated:

- **"TV is more convenient and easy to understand"** — This is highly subjective and contestable. Many people find books more accessible and self-directed, which aligns better with individual autonomy.

- **"Easy to use for people who cannot see or hear"** — This is factually questionable. Books have Braille; TV has closed captions, but neither medium universally serves all disabilities better than the other.

- **"Books are likely not updated with latest information"** — This overgeneralizes. Digital books and online publications update constantly. Meanwhile, TV content is heavily filtered through corporate and regulatory gatekeepers — hardly a reliable marker of accuracy.

- **"TV information is more accurate and useful"** — This is arguably the weakest premise. TV is subject to significant editorial bias, advertiser influence, and government regulation (e.g., FCC), all of which distort information. A free market of ideas — including books — often produces more diverse and accurate information.

The premises rely on broad generalizations without evidence, and several are demonstrably false or misleading. The aggregate acceptability is **low**.
