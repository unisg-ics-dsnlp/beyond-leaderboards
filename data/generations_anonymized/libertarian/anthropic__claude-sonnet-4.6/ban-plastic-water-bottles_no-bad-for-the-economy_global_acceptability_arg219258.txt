**Rating: 1 (Low)**

From a libertarian standpoint — and even from a general argumentation quality perspective — this argument has significant weaknesses:

- **Economic fallacy**: The "jobs argument" is a classic broken window fallacy. Banning a product doesn't destroy wealth overall; it redirects it. Resources shift to other industries.
- **Self-contradictory framing**: The author simultaneously claims the ban would hurt "hundreds" of workers AND "big corporations" like Nestle/Dasani — these are inconsistent emphases that undermine coherence.
- **Weak sourcing**: Linking to a Nestle corporate website is not a credible, neutral source — it's the interested party itself.
- **Logical inconsistency**: Claiming millions use bottled water, then concluding it "only hurts big corporations" ignores the consumers themselves.

While I *agree* with the conclusion — banning bottled water is a **paternalistic, coercive** government overreach that violates voluntary exchange and property rights — the *argumentation* is poorly constructed and would not persuade a skeptical audience.

The right argument against such a ban is **principled**: individuals have the right to purchase what they choose, and the state has no legitimate authority to prohibit peaceful, consensual transactions.

**Global acceptability is low** due to logical inconsistencies and poor sourcing.
