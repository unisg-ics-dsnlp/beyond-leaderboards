The argument essentially states: "We cannot ban water bottles *until* we exit the recession."

The premise here is the **economic recession** — implying that economic conditions should determine whether a prohibition policy is enacted.

From my libertarian lens, this premise is **largely irrelevant** to the conclusion for a key reason:

- The recession premise doesn't actually address *whether* banning water bottles is ever justified in the first place. It merely **delays** a coercive policy rather than questioning its legitimacy.
- A principled argument against banning water bottles would rest on **property rights**, **voluntary exchange**, and the **non-aggression principle** — none of which are recession-dependent.
- The premise implicitly **concedes** that a ban becomes acceptable once economic conditions improve, which is a fundamentally weak and irrelevant foundation for either supporting or opposing such a prohibition.

The economic condition of society has little logical bearing on whether the state has the right to prohibit individuals from purchasing or using water bottles. The premise simply doesn't do meaningful work in establishing the conclusion.
