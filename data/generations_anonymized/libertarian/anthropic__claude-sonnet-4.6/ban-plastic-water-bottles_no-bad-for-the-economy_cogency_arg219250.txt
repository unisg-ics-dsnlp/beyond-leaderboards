I'd rate this argument **2 (Medium)** for cogency.

**Reasoning:**

The argument has a reasonable structure — it concedes a premise (bottled water is wasteful) and offers a counter-consideration (reusability). From a libertarian lens, this is actually a **voluntary market solution**: consumers choosing to reuse bottles rather than being coerced into bans or mandates.

However, the argument is **only partially cogent**:

- The premise "bottled water is a waste" is **accepted but underexamined** — waste relative to what standard?
- The rebuttal ("bottles can be reused") is **relevant** but **insufficiently developed** — it doesn't establish *how often* reuse actually occurs, or whether it meaningfully offsets the alleged waste.
- The conclusion is **implied rather than stated**, weakening logical sufficiency.

The argument gestures toward a valid point — that **individual behavioral choices** (reuse) can address environmental concerns without coercive intervention — but it lacks the rigor to fully support that conclusion.

**Medium cogency** is appropriate: the logic is directionally sound but underdeveloped.
