**Rating: 2 (Medium)**

The argument makes some reasonable points — accessibility for those with sensory impairments and timeliness of information are legitimate considerations. These are relevant to the debate and broadly acceptable claims.

However, the reasoning has notable weaknesses:

- The claim that TV information is "more accurate" is highly contestable — books often undergo rigorous editorial review, while TV content varies wildly in quality.
- "Convenience" and "ease of understanding" are subjective and don't necessarily make TV *better* in an educational sense — depth and critical engagement matter too.
- The argument overgeneralizes, treating all TV and all books as monolithic categories.

From my perspective, the deeper issue is one of **voluntary choice** — individuals should be free to consume information however they prefer, and the market will reflect those preferences. But judged purely on argumentative quality, this is a **medium-effort argument**: relevant but insufficiently rigorous.
