**Rating: 2 (Medium)**

The statement presents a simple factual claim — that the Foxtel Go app allows iPad users to watch TV anywhere, anytime. As an argument, it's straightforward: the premise (app availability on iPad) is meant to support the conclusion (flexible viewing freedom).

From a cogency standpoint:

- **Acceptability**: The premise is plausible and likely true, but unverified here.
- **Relevance**: The premise is relevant — app availability does logically connect to viewing flexibility.
- **Sufficiency**: This is where it weakens. The argument omits critical conditions — a **subscription requirement**, internet connectivity, geographic restrictions, and content licensing limitations. These gaps mean the premise alone is **insufficient** to fully support the sweeping conclusion of "wherever and whenever you want."

From my libertarian lens, I'd also note this is essentially a **voluntary market transaction** — a consumer choosing a service they consent to pay for — which is perfectly legitimate. But the argument oversells the freedom it delivers by glossing over real constraints.

Hence, **Medium (2)** — the argument has merit but lacks sufficient completeness to rate highly.
