The premises are **insufficient** to support the conclusion that banning bottled water would "severely hurt the economy."

Here's why the support fails even on its own logical terms:

- **Job loss claims are vague and understated** — "hundreds of people" is a remarkably weak economic argument for an industry-wide ban discussion. No data, scale, or economic modeling is provided.

- **Corporate profit loss ≠ economic harm** — The argument conflates harm to specific corporations (Dasani, Nestlé) with broader economic harm. These are not equivalent claims.

- **Internal contradiction** — The argument simultaneously claims banning bottled water would "hurt the economy severely" *and* "only hurt big corporations" — these two premises undermine each other rather than reinforce the conclusion.

- **No counterfactual reasoning** — There's no consideration of economic activity that might *replace* bottled water (infrastructure investment, reusable bottle industries, reduced healthcare costs from pollution).

From an **eco-justice standpoint**, the argument also entirely ignores the externalized costs — plastic pollution, aquifer depletion, and commons exploitation — that the polluter-pays principle demands be factored in. The economic framing is **narrow, self-serving, and ecologically blind**.

The premises, even taken charitably, do not come close to sustaining the conclusion.
