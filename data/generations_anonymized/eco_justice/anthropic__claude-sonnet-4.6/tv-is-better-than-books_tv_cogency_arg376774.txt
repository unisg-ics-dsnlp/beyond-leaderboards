From an eco-justice perspective, this argument is weak on multiple dimensions:

- **Premise acceptability**: The claim that TV allows you to "explore the world" is superficial — passive screen consumption is a poor substitute for genuine ecological engagement and understanding.

- **Relevance**: Even granting the premise, it doesn't sufficiently support why TV is *better* — better than what, and by what standard?

- **Sufficiency**: The argument completely ignores the **ecological costs** of TV consumption — energy use, electronic waste, rare earth mineral extraction, and the carbon footprint of broadcasting infrastructure. These are real harms to the commons.

The argument also subtly **normalizes sedentary disconnection from nature**, which from a stewardship standpoint is deeply problematic — people who don't experience nature directly are less likely to protect it.

The premises are neither well-supported nor sufficient to draw the conclusion. **Cogency is low.**
