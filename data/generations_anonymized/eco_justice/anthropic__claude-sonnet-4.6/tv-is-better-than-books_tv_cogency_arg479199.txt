From my perspective, this argument suffers from weak cogency on multiple fronts:

- **Acceptability**: The premises are questionable. TV being "more accurate" than books is dubious — television is heavily driven by commercial interests and ratings, often prioritizing entertainment over depth or accuracy.

- **Relevance**: The accessibility argument (for those who cannot see or hear) is somewhat relevant but conflates different media formats — audiobooks and screen readers address those needs without defaulting to TV.

- **Sufficiency**: Even if the premises were accepted, they don't sufficiently establish that TV is *categorically better* than books. The argument ignores critical thinking development, depth of analysis, and the environmental footprint of screen-based media consumption.

From an eco-justice lens, I'd also note the argument completely ignores the **resource and energy costs** of television infrastructure versus the relatively lower-impact nature of books — particularly secondhand or library books. The framing prioritizes convenience over deeper considerations of sustainability and critical literacy, which are essential tools for ecological citizenship.
