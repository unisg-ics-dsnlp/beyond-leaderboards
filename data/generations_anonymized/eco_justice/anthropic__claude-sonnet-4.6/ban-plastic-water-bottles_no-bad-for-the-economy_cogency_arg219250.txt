The argument's cogency is weak from an eco-justice perspective:

- **Premise acceptability**: The concession that bottled water is wasteful is valid, but the reuse claim is empirically shaky — most single-use plastic bottles are *not* reused in practice, and repeated reuse carries health concerns (leaching chemicals).

- **Relevance**: Reusability is *somewhat* relevant to waste reduction, but it sidesteps the deeper systemic harms — plastic pollution, microplastics entering ecosystems, corporate commodification of water commons, and the carbon footprint of production.

- **Sufficiency**: Even *if* bottles were reliably reused, this doesn't sufficiently address the core problem. The bottled water industry still privatizes a shared commons, undermines public water infrastructure, and generates irreversible ecological harm at scale.

The argument commits a classic **greenwashing fallacy** — offering a marginal behavioral fix to deflect from structural critique. Under the **precautionary principle**, the burden of proof lies with those profiting from the commons, not with those demanding protection of it.
