**Rating: 1 (Low)**

The argumentation is weak on multiple fronts from an eco-justice perspective:

- **"Help us"** is vague and unsubstantiated — help *whom*, exactly? The plastic industry? Certainly not ecosystems or future generations.
- The historical pollution framing **normalizes** rather than challenges the root cause. Industrial contamination of waterways is a *failure* of stewardship, not a justification for perpetuating single-use plastic consumption.
- The argument **shifts the burden** — instead of demanding clean public water infrastructure (the just solution), it implicitly accepts privatized, plastic-packaged water as the answer.
- There is **no engagement** with the ecological costs of bottled water: plastic pollution, carbon footprint, extraction of groundwater commons.
- The "cultural" explanation is superficial and deflects accountability from polluters who should bear remediation costs under the **polluter-pays principle**.

The argument essentially launders ecological harm as cultural necessity — precisely the kind of reasoning that perpetuates irreversible damage to commons and shifts burdens onto future generations. That is a red line for me.
