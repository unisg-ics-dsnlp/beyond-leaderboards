**Rating: 1 (Low)**

The argument fails on multiple cogency dimensions:

- **Acceptability**: The premise that European bottled water consumption stems from Industrial Revolution pollution is historically oversimplified and poorly supported. Tap water quality in Western Europe is now among the highest globally.

- **Relevance**: Even if the cultural-historical premise were true, it doesn't logically connect to the conclusion that bottled water purchases "help us" — who is "us"? The argument is vague and incomplete.

- **Sufficiency**: The premises are wholly insufficient to justify the conclusion. Bottled water consumption at that scale generates **massive plastic waste**, burdens commons (aquifers, oceans), and shifts ecological costs onto future generations — the opposite of "helping."

From an eco-justice standpoint, this argument also obscures the **polluter-pays principle**: industries that historically degraded water sources should bear remediation costs, not consumers perpetuating a plastic-dependent cycle. The framing normalizes ecological harm rather than addressing root causes.

The argument is cognitively weak and ideologically problematic.
