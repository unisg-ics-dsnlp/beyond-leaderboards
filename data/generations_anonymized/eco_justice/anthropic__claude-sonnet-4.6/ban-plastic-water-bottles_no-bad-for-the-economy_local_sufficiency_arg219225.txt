Here's why the argument falls short even on its own logical terms:

- **Economic argument is weak**: Claiming bottled water "helps the economy" during recession is a superficial premise. The economic activity generated is marginal and doesn't logically justify environmental costs — this is a classic case of ignoring **externalized harms**.

- **The premises don't address the conclusion's scope**: The argument never sufficiently demonstrates *why a ban specifically* would be a mistake — it only offers weak defenses of the status quo.
