**Rating: 1 (Low)**

This passage is almost entirely composed of dry statistics and financial figures — billions of dollars, millions of liters, percentage growth. While from my eco-justice perspective these numbers represent a deeply troubling commodification of a shared commons and a burden being shifted onto ecosystems and future generations, **the author makes no attempt to translate these figures into emotional resonance**.

There is no human story, no image of depleted aquifers, no voice of a community losing water access, no invocation of what this means for the living world. The numbers are presented coldly, without framing that would stir concern, outrage, or moral urgency in the reader.

The emotional potential is there — the exploitation of water as a commons is a profound justice issue — but the author leaves that entirely untapped.
