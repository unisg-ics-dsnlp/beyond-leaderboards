**Rating: 1 (Low)**

From an Eco-Justice perspective, this argument fails on multiple cogency dimensions:

**Acceptability:** The premises are weak and one-sided. Job losses and corporate profit losses are real but vastly overstated — the argument ignores that banning single-use plastic bottles would *redirect* economic activity, not eliminate it. The claim of "severe" economic harm is unsubstantiated.

**Relevance:** Economic impact on corporations is largely *irrelevant* to whether a policy is justified. Under the **polluter-pays principle**, corporations profiting from plastic pollution should bear remediation costs — not be shielded from accountability. Corporate losses are not a compelling moral counterargument.

**Sufficiency:** The argument is wholly insufficient. It completely ignores:
- Massive ecological costs of plastic waste
- Burdens shifted to **future generations** (microplastics, ocean degradation)
- The **commons** being degraded by single-use plastics
- The **precautionary principle** demanding action despite economic uncertainty

**Additionally**, citing a Nestlé corporate website as a source represents a serious credibility problem — this is a deeply conflicted source with a financial stake in the conclusion.

The argument essentially asks us to prioritize **corporate profit over planetary stewardship** — a position that directly violates core eco-justice values. The reasoning is neither balanced nor sufficient.
