**Rating: 1 (Low)**

The argument has significant acceptability problems from multiple angles:

- **Factually dubious**: The claim that bottled water "helps us" is vague and unsupported. It obscures *who* benefits and at what cost.
- **Deflects systemic responsibility**: Framing chronic water pollution as a *cultural justification* for continued bottled water consumption rather than demanding remediation violates the **polluter pays** principle — industries that polluted water sources should bear cleanup costs, not normalize the harm.
- **Ignores ecological costs**: Bottled water production generates plastic waste, carbon emissions, and resource extraction harms — shifting burdens onto ecosystems and future generations.
- **Logical gap**: Even if historical pollution explains *why* Europeans drink bottled water, it does not justify the practice as acceptable or beneficial globally.

Most critically, the argument would **not** be broadly accepted even outside an eco-justice framework — it conflates explanation with justification and makes an unsubstantiated positive claim ("helps us"). A general audience would likely find it poorly reasoned and incomplete.
