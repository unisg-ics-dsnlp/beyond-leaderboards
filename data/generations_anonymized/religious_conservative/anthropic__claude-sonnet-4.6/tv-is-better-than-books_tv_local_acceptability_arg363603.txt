**Rating: 2 (Medium)**

The argument contains premises that are partially acceptable but lack nuance. The claim that television offers convenient access to diverse informational content is **factually reasonable** — documentaries and educational channels do exist and provide accessible learning.

However, from my worldview, several premises deserve scrutiny:

- The implicit assumption that **ease and convenience** make something *better* is questionable. Books cultivate discipline, deep reflection, and virtue — qualities that passive television viewing rarely fosters.
- The premise that **entertainment on demand** is a straightforward good overlooks the moral quality of what is consumed. "Whatever you want to watch" is not a neutral statement — it assumes personal preference is the primary standard, which conflicts with the principle that our leisure choices should also be ordered toward the good.
