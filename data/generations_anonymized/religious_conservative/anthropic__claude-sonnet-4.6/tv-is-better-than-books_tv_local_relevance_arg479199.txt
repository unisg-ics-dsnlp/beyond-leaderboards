**Rating: 2 (Medium)**

The premises offered — convenience, accessibility for those with sensory impairments, and currency of information — are *partially* relevant to the conclusion that TV is superior to books. They do contribute genuine reasons worth considering.

However, from my perspective, the argument is notably incomplete and therefore only moderately relevant in aggregate:

- **Convenience** is a relevant but shallow criterion. Ease does not equal value — inherited wisdom often requires *effort* to internalize, and that effort builds character and discipline.
- **Accessibility** is a genuinely relevant point, particularly given our duty to protect and include the vulnerable, though it addresses a narrow subset of cases.
- **Currency of information** is relevant but overstated — books remain authoritative repositories of enduring moral, historical, and theological knowledge that does not require constant updating.

The premises address *practical utility* but largely ignore deeper dimensions of what education is *for* — the formation of virtue, moral reasoning, and transmission of tradition. Since the argument's premises only partially engage with what truly matters in comparing these media, **Medium relevance** is the appropriate rating.
