**Rating: 2 (Medium)**

The argument contains premises that are partially acceptable but deserve scrutiny from a principled standpoint.

**What holds up:** It is factually true that television conveys visual and emotional immediacy — seeing disaster survivors does carry emotional weight. This is not deniable.

**Where I have reservations:** The premise that "books take too much time" and that speed equals superiority reflects a troubling modern bias. Our inherited wisdom — Scripture, great literature, moral philosophy — was transmitted precisely through the *slow* medium of the written word. Depth, reflection, and virtue formation require patience, not efficiency.

Furthermore, the emotional power of television is a double-edged sword. Vivid imagery can manipulate as easily as it informs, bypassing reason and conscience rather than forming them.

The premises are **partially true but philosophically shallow**, hence a **medium** rating.
