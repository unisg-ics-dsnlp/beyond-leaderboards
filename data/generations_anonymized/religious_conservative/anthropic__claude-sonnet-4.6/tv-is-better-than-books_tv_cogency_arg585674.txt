**Rating: 2 (Medium)**

The argument presents two sub-arguments: (1) television's visual and auditory immediacy makes information more powerful than reading, and (2) TV conveys information faster than books.

The premises are generally **acceptable** — visual media does carry emotional immediacy, and TV does deliver information quickly. The disaster example is relatable and concrete.

However, the argument suffers from **sufficiency problems**. Speed and emotional impact do not alone establish TV's superiority as an information medium. From my perspective, there is profound value in the **depth, reflection, and wisdom** that written tradition cultivates — qualities essential to forming virtuous, well-reasoned citizens. Faster and more emotionally stimulating does not mean *better*.

The argument also conflates **emotional power with informational quality**, which is a meaningful logical gap. Cogency requires that premises be *sufficient* to support the conclusion, and here they fall short of establishing a comprehensive case.

It is a moderately coherent argument with real but limited force — hence **Medium**.
