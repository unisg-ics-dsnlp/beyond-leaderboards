**Rating: 2 (Medium)**

The premises offer *some* support for the conclusion that television is superior to books for conveying information. The emotional immediacy argument — that visual and auditory storytelling is more powerful than text — has genuine merit and is reasonably supported by the disaster coverage example.

However, the premises are only partially sufficient. The argument conflates *speed and emotional impact* with *overall informational value*, which are not the same thing. A book or written account may convey deeper context, moral reflection, and wisdom that a brief television segment cannot. The premise that "books take too much time" is asserted rather than demonstrated, and it assumes that speed is the primary measure of informational worth — a debatable claim.

The conclusion that TV is broadly superior is not fully warranted by these premises alone.
