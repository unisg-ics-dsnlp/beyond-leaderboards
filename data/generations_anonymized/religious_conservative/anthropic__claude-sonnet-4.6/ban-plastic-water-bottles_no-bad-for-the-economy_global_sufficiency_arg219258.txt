**Rating: 1 (Low)**

From my perspective, this argument fails significantly on global sufficiency. It makes an economic case against banning bottled water but leaves virtually every major counter-argument unaddressed:

- **Environmental stewardship** — a duty rooted in our responsibility as caretakers of God's creation — is entirely ignored. The harm plastic waste causes to communities and nature is a serious moral objection left unrebutted.

- **Common good vs. corporate profit** — the argument essentially defends large corporations' revenues as the primary concern, which is a weak moral foundation. It never addresses whether those economic interests outweigh broader community health and environmental duties.

- **Subsidiarity and local alternatives** — no engagement with whether local water infrastructure or reusable alternatives could replace jobs and serve communities better.

- **The vulnerable** — no consideration of how plastic pollution disproportionately harms poorer communities and children.

The argument is narrow, self-referential, and defends powerful corporate interests without acknowledging the legitimate moral and communal objections one would naturally anticipate. A truly sufficient argument must engage its strongest critics — this one does not.
