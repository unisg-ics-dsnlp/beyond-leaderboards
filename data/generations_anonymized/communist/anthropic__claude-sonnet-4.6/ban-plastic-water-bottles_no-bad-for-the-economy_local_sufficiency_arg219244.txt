The premises are insufficient to support the conclusion. The argument attempts to link European water consumption patterns to historical industrial pollution, but this chain of reasoning has critical gaps:

- The claim that European water pollution "dating back to the Industrial Revolution" explains *current* bottled water consumption is asserted without evidence — modern water treatment has vastly improved tap water quality in Western Europe.
- The leap from "Europeans buy bottled water" to "the water bottles **help us**" is entirely unsupported — who is "us"? How does their consumption help? This conclusion is essentially **unargued**.
- The cultural explanation offered is vague and competes with other plausible explanations (marketing, habit, taste preference) that are not addressed.

From a materialist perspective, one might also note the argument **obscures class dynamics** — bottled water is a commodity produced for profit, and its mass consumption reflects capitalist market creation rather than genuine necessity in most Western European contexts.

The premises, even if accepted, simply do not provide sufficient logical support for the conclusion drawn.
