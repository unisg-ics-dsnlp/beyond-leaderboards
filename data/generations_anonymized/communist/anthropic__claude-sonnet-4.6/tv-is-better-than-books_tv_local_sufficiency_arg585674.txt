**Rating: 2 (Medium)**

The premises offer *some* support for the conclusion that TV is superior to books for conveying information, but they are insufficient on their own.

The argument correctly identifies that visual media can create emotional immediacy — seeing disaster survivors is indeed more viscerally impactful than reading descriptions. This premise reasonably supports the conclusion about TV's power.

However, the second premise — that books "take too much time" — is underdeveloped. Speed of consumption does not automatically equal better understanding or more valuable information. From a materialist perspective, one might note that *depth of analysis* matters enormously; understanding *why* disasters occur, or the class dimensions of who suffers most, requires more than immediate visual impressions.

The premises together provide *partial* but not *sufficient* support — they address emotional impact and speed but ignore comprehension, critical analysis, and depth, which are relevant to the broader implicit conclusion that TV is simply *better* than books for information.
