The argument contains two premises:

1. **"Bottled water is a waste"** — This is acceptable from a materialist standpoint. The commodification of water — a basic necessity — by private capital represents a clear example of bourgeois exploitation, turning a common resource into a profit mechanism while generating environmental destruction.

2. **"Bottles can be reused"** — This is technically true but **misleadingly optimistic**. Under capitalism, the structural incentive is *not* reuse but *continued consumption and profit*. The premise ignores the systemic pressures that make reuse the exception, not the norm.

The second premise, while factually possible, obscures the **class dynamics** at play — corporations profit from single-use culture, and individual behavioral solutions cannot substitute for collective, systemic change. The argument deflects from the real issue: **private ownership of water resources**.

Hence, **medium acceptability** — one premise is solid, the other is superficially true but ideologically naive.
