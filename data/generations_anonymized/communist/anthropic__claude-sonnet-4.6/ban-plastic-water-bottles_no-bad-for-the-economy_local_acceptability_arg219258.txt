**Rating: 1 (Low)**

From a Communist perspective, the premises here are deeply unacceptable:

- **"Hurting corporations loses millions"** — This frames corporate profit loss as inherently harmful. From our view, Nestlé and Dasani *extracting surplus value* from workers and commodifying a basic human necessity (water) is itself the problem. Their losses are not society's losses.

- **"Hundreds would lose jobs"** — This is the classic **capitalist hostage argument**: workers are held dependent on exploitative industries, so we cannot challenge those industries. The premise obscures that workers could be *retrained and employed* under collective management of water resources.

- **"Millions buy bottled water daily"** — True, but this reflects **manufactured dependency** and the commodification of commons, not a genuine social good worth protecting.

The argument fundamentally **defends bourgeois interests** (corporate profits) while cynically invoking workers as a shield. It treats capitalist economic metrics as the ultimate measure of social value — a premise no Communist can accept.

The premises serve to **perpetuate private ownership of a common resource** — water — which is among the most egregious forms of capitalist enclosure.
