The premises are **acceptable** — it is true that books tend to be in dominant languages and TV offers multilingual content with pronunciation guidance.

However, the argument is only **partially sufficient**:
- It correctly identifies TV as more linguistically accessible than books
- The relevance is clear — it supports the implicit conclusion that TV has advantages for language learners in migration contexts

The **weakness** lies in sufficiency — the argument is somewhat thin. It does not account for:
- The class dimension of *who controls* media content and *whose* languages are represented
- Whether TV truly serves the linguistic needs of migrant workers or merely reflects the dominant culture's languages
- The broader structural reasons why migrants face these barriers in the first place

The premises support the conclusion, but only **moderately well** — the reasoning is straightforward but incomplete, lacking depth to fully establish the conclusion.
