The premise — that TV delivers information faster from multiple sources compared to books — is **locally relevant** to the conclusion that TV is superior for information gathering. It does contribute a genuine reason supporting the claim.

However, from a materialist perspective, this argument is **superficial**: it ignores *whose* information is being broadcast, *who controls* the means of media production, and *what class interests* shape that content. Speed and volume of information are real considerations, but they are secondary premises that only partially support the conclusion without addressing deeper questions of quality, critical thinking, or ideological bias embedded in media ownership.

The premise is relevant but **incomplete**, hence **Medium (2)**.
