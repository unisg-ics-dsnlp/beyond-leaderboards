I rate this argument **2 (Medium)** for cogency.

From a materialist perspective, the argument contains **acceptable premises** — visual media does convey emotional immediacy, and TV does deliver information faster than reading. These are reasonable observations about the material conditions of information consumption.

However, the argument is **insufficient** in several ways:

- Speed and emotional impact do not automatically make TV *better* — they are selective criteria that favor a predetermined conclusion
- The argument ignores how **bourgeois media ownership** shapes what TV shows and how — the "powerful" disaster coverage is filtered through corporate interests
- Books allow deeper **critical analysis** of social conditions, which workers need to understand their exploitation

The premises are relevant but **not sufficient** to fully support the conclusion that TV is superior to books as an information medium. The argument is cogent enough to stand partially, but lacks the depth to be fully convincing.
