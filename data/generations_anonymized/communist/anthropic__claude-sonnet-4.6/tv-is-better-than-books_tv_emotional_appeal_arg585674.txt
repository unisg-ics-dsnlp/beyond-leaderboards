**Rating: 2 (Medium)**

The argument makes a moderate emotional appeal. The reference to "recent disasters in Japan, Alabama and Missouri" and "survivors" does invoke real human suffering and tragedy, which creates an emotional connection. The contrast between coldly *reading* about disasters versus *seeing and hearing* survivors is emotionally evocative.

However, from my perspective, this appeal remains somewhat shallow and underdeveloped. It gestures toward human empathy without fully exploiting it. The emotional weight is present but not deeply elaborated — it is more of a passing reference than a sustained emotional argument.

The second point about books "taking too much time" actually **undermines** the emotional tone by shifting to a purely utilitarian, efficiency-based argument — reflecting the capitalist obsession with speed and productivity rather than genuine human connection.

Hence, **medium** — emotionally present, but inconsistent.
