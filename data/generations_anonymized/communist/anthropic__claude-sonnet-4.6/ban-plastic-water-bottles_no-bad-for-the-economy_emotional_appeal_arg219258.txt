**Rating: 1 (Low)**

From a Communist perspective, this argument attempts emotional appeal by invoking job losses and economic harm — but it fails even on its own terms.

The argument cynically conflates **worker interests with corporate interests**. Mentioning "hundreds of jobs" alongside "millions in losses for Dasani and Nestlé" exposes the contradiction — the workers and the bourgeoisie are not the same class. This muddling actually **weakens** emotional resonance because it asks the audience to feel sympathy for exploitative corporations like Nestlé, which has a well-documented history of commodifying public water resources.

The emotional appeal is shallow and transparent — it essentially asks workers to defend the very corporations that exploit them. Most readers, even outside a Communist framework, can sense this manipulation, which undermines rather than strengthens emotional engagement.

The argument generates **no genuine solidarity or empathy** — it merely serves as ideological cover for capital interests.
