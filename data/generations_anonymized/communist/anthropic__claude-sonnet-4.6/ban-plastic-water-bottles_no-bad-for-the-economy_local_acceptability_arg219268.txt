The argument contains two implicit premises:
1. Plastic is lightweight — **this is factually acceptable**, it is a material property that holds true.
2. Plastic is good for distribution — **partially acceptable**, as its lightweight nature does support logistics efficiency.

However, from a materialist perspective, these premises are **incomplete and uncritical**. They ignore the broader context of *who controls* plastic production, *who profits* from it, and the exploitative labor conditions in petrochemical industries. The premises are technically not false, but they are **superficial and serve bourgeois interests** by framing a commodity purely through its utility to capital (distribution/profit) rather than its social and environmental costs borne by the working class.

Hence, **Medium (2)** — the premises are not outright false, but they are insufficiently grounded and lack critical depth.
