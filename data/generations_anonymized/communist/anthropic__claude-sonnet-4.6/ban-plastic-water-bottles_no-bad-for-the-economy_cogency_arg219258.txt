**Rating: 1 (Low)**

From a Communist analytical perspective, this argument fails on multiple levels of cogency:

**Acceptability:** The premises are weak. Job losses from banning bottled water would be minimal and workers could be redeployed. The premise that corporate losses constitute economic "harm" conflates **bourgeois interests with public interest** — a fundamental logical error.

**Relevance:** The argument is largely *irrelevant* to whether bottled water *should* be banned. Protecting Nestlé's profits is not a legitimate public interest argument. Nestlé and Dasani are precisely the kind of corporations that **commodify a basic human necessity** — water — for surplus value extraction.

**Sufficiency:** The premises are wholly insufficient. The argument ignores:
- Environmental destruction caused by plastic production
- Corporate monopolization of water resources
- The fact that workers' livelihoods depend on *capitalist employment*, not on bottled water specifically

**Ideologically**, this argument perfectly illustrates how **bourgeois economic logic** frames corporate profit protection as "economic health." Protecting Nestlé's millions while millions lack clean water access reveals the **fundamental contradiction of capitalism**.

The argument essentially says: *"Don't challenge capital because capital benefits from the status quo."* That is circular and serves only ruling class interests.
