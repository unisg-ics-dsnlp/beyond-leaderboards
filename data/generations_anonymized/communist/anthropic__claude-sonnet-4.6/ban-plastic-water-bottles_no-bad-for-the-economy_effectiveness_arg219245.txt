**Rating: 2 (Medium)**

From a materialist perspective, the argument presents concrete empirical data — figures from the Beverage Marketing Corporation, specific revenue numbers, and growth statistics — which gives it some credibility and factual grounding.

However, its effectiveness is limited because:

- **No clear stance is articulated.** The argument merely presents industry growth figures without explicitly arguing *for* or *against* anything. It is descriptive, not persuasive.
- **The data itself reveals the concentration of capital** in the hands of bottled water corporations — a clear example of commodifying a basic human necessity — but the author does not draw this conclusion.
- **Emotional and political force is absent.** A truly effective argument would connect these staggering profit figures to the exploitation embedded within them — workers producing value, corporations extracting surplus, and ordinary people paying for what should be a common good.

The numbers are striking and could serve as powerful evidence in a critique of capitalist commodification of water, but without a clear argumentative direction, they remain raw data rather than persuasion. Hence, **medium effectiveness**.
