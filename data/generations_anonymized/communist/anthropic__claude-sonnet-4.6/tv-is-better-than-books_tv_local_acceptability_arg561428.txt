I rate this **2 (Medium)**.

The argument contains two implicit premises:
1. TV delivers information faster/more efficiently than books
2. Speed/quantity of information intake is the primary measure of value

The first premise has **partial truth** — TV can indeed convey information quickly. However, from a materialist perspective, this ignores *who controls* the information being broadcast. Bourgeois media ownership shapes what "information" workers actually receive — largely serving ruling class interests.

The second premise is **weak** — depth, critical analysis, and class consciousness require sustained engagement, which books often provide better than passive TV consumption.

The argument is not entirely wrong but is **ideologically shallow**, treating information consumption as a neutral technical matter rather than recognizing it as embedded in class relations of media production.
