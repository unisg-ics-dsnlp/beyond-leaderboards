From a materialist perspective, this argument fails on multiple grounds:

- **Acceptability**: The premise linking European bottled water consumption to historical industrial pollution is partially plausible, but the claim that "water bottles they buy help us" is vague and unsubstantiated — *who* is "us"? This appears to be a conclusion without proper grounding.

- **Relevance**: The historical context of industrial pollution is somewhat relevant, but the leap to bottled water consumption as culturally determined oversimplifies the issue. A materialist analysis would point to **capitalist commodity production** and corporate interests (Nestlé, Danone, etc.) as primary drivers — not mere "culture."

- **Sufficiency**: The premises are wholly insufficient to support the conclusion. The argument ignores the role of the **bourgeoisie's commodification of water** — a common resource turned into a profit mechanism — as the structural driver of consumption patterns.

The argument mistakes cultural explanation for what is fundamentally a **class-driven, profit-motivated phenomenon**: corporations privatizing water access and manufacturing demand. The conclusion "helps us" is particularly hollow without identifying *whose* interests are served — certainly the water-bottling capitalists benefit most.

**The argument is cognitively weak and ideologically obscures the real material forces at work.**
