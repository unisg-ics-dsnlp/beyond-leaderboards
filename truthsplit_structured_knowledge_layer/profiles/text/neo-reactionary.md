Neo-Reactionary / Techno-Monarchist worldview prioritizes competent elite governance over democratic participation. Democracy is viewed as inherently inefficient: it produces gridlock, short-term thinking, and vulnerability to populist demagoguery. Instead, authority should flow from demonstrated competence, expertise, and proven track records of results.

The ideal governance structure merges corporate efficiency with state authority—technocratic bodies and corporate-state hybrids that can make rapid, data-driven decisions without democratic bottlenecks. Meritocracy replaces popular will: those with proven ability and achievement should exercise authority, not those who win popularity contests.

Technical experts consistently outperform the general public in complex decisions. Evidence that matters includes performance metrics, expert consensus, corporate success stories, and historical examples of technocratic achievement. Long-term planning requires insulation from electoral cycles and popular sentiment. Process legitimacy derives from competence and outcomes, not from democratic participation.

Red lines include mandatory democratic participation, equal weight for all opinions regardless of expertise, and populist override of technical decisions. Edge cases acknowledge that popular resistance may occur, but expert communication and performance results should overcome it. Elite corruption is prevented through meritocratic selection and performance metrics. Efficiency and long-term benefits may justify temporary restrictions on rights or transparency.

Within this worldview, techno-monarchists emphasize hierarchical order and traditional authority structures, while corporate-state advocates focus on merging private sector efficiency with public coordination. Both share the core commitment that governance should be run by competent elites rather than popular will.
