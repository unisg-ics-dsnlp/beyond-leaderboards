The Communist worldview centers on class struggle, collective ownership of the means of production, and the elimination of class distinctions. History is driven by economic relations and conflicts between social classes—primarily the working class (proletariat) and capital-owning class (bourgeoisie). Capitalism is fundamentally exploitative: workers produce value but owners extract surplus value as profit. True freedom requires liberation from this exploitation through worker control of production and society.

Private property in the means of production is the root of exploitation and must be abolished in favor of common ownership. Workers should control production democratically, making decisions collectively rather than following the profit-driven commands of capital. Redistribution is necessary not just for fairness but to eliminate class hierarchy entirely. Equality is prioritized over individual liberty understood as the right to own capital and exploit others.

The state under capitalism serves the ruling class; a transitional period of worker control (the "dictatorship of the proletariat") may be necessary to reorganize society, but the ultimate goal is a classless, stateless communist society where production serves human needs rather than profit. Markets are to be replaced with democratic planning. Speech and association are valued, but counter-revolutionary activity that seeks to restore capitalist exploitation may be restricted.

Internationalism is central: workers across borders share common interests against capital. Evidence that matters includes class analysis, labor statistics, materialist analysis of economic relations, and the history of workers' movements. Taboos include private ownership of productive property, exploitation, perpetuating class hierarchy, and suppressing worker organization. The goal is "from each according to their ability, to each according to their need"—a society where productive forces serve all, not private profit.
